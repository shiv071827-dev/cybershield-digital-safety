FIELD NOTE / 08 SIGNALS

Patterns to recognise

Educational patterns, not incident reports or statistics.

01

AI-powered impersonation

Cloned voices and synthetic profiles can make an unexpected request feel familiar.

PRACTICAL CHECKCall back using a number you already have, not one supplied with the request.

02

QR and payment manipulation

A QR code or collect request can initiate a payment. Read the payment screen and confirm the recipient; a PIN authorises money leaving, not arriving.

PRACTICAL CHECKCheck the recipient and amount in your payment app; never enter a PIN to receive money.

03

Social engineering

Urgency, authority, fear or affection can pressure people to act before checking.

PRACTICAL CHECKPause and verify the request through contact details you find independently.

04

Fake digital support

Fraudulent support listings may promise refunds or account fixes, then ask for codes, remote access or payment.

PRACTICAL CHECKFind help through the organisation's official app or website.

05

Investment and job fraud

Fake vacancies can demand upfront fees; investment pitches may promise certainty or unusually high returns.

PRACTICAL CHECKVerify employers, firms and claims with official sources before paying or investing.

06

Deepfake and synthetic media

Manipulated audio, photos and video can create a false sense of evidence. A convincing recording is not proof.

PRACTICAL CHECKConfirm unusual claims through a separate channel you already trust.

01 / PHISHING

Phishing & suspicious links

Urgent account warnings and unfamiliar links deserve a second look. Open the official app or website yourself to check.

02 / UPI & QR

UPI & QR requests

A UPI PIN authorises a payment; it is not needed to receive money. Review unexpected requests in your payment app.

03 / SUPPORT

Fake customer care

Find support through an organisation's official app or verified website. Do not install tools at a caller's request.

04 / OPPORTUNITY

Job & scholarship scams

Check offers independently, especially when they promise guaranteed selection or demand an upfront fee.

05 / ACCESS

OTP, PIN & remote access

Keep verification codes and PINs private. Never grant unexpected screen access or disclose a code to a caller.

06 / IDENTITY

Impersonation

Verify urgent requests through a known, separate contact route before sharing information or sending money.

07 / INVESTMENT

Investment fraud

Guaranteed returns and pressure to transfer quickly are warning signs. Check claims with official sources.

08 / SYNTHETIC MEDIA

AI & synthetic deception

A familiar-sounding voice is not proof of identity. Confirm unusual requests through a contact route you already trust.

ⓘFor awareness only. Scenarios are fictional and are not instructions to interact with suspicious messages.

03A PRACTICAL SAFETY PROTOCOL

If it feels wrong,
take the safe route.

A calm checklist can help students, families, and older adults respond without panic. You never need to share a password, OTP, PIN, or banking details to report a scam.

01

Stop contact

Do not click, reply, call back, install an app, or approve an unexpected payment request.

02

Verify independently

Use the official app or a number you already trust—not the link or number in the suspicious message.

03

Secure and document

Save relevant messages and transaction references. Change exposed credentials using the official service.

04

Report promptly

For financial cyber fraud in India, call 1930 promptly and notify your bank using its official contact.

BEFORE YOU REPORT

Keep yourself safer

Save relevant messages, transaction references, and dates. Never share your OTP, PIN, password, or full card details with anyone.

Use official resources↗

Keep useful details for a report

  • Transaction IDs, payment references, dates, and times.
  • Screenshots, messages, emails, and suspicious phone numbers.
  • Relevant email headers when reporting an email.

Official information can change. Check the Government of India portal for current reporting steps. CyberShield is an educational project, not an emergency service.
